## 🏆 Video 2: Local Judging Pitch Script (240 seconds max / 4:00)
> *The four blocks follow the judging guide: WHO (45s), WHY (60s), WHAT (60s), and SO WHAT NEXT (75s). Rehearse with the demo pauses; finish before 4:00.*

### **BLOCK 1: WHO & THE HOOK (0:00 – 0:45 | 45 seconds)**
> **[On camera: the team; show the Jr_AstroCamp title.]**
> *"Hello, everyone. We’re Team Mysterio, and this is Jr_AstroCamp: Fly. Learn. Explore. Becoming an astronaut can take years of training—and for most students, a lifetime of waiting for a seat. What if they could fly a mission today, make real engineering decisions, and learn safely from a crash? Our six-person team brings together AI automation, data science, machine learning, full-stack development, research, and UI/UX around one idea: make the mission itself the classroom."*

---

### **BLOCK 2: WHY & THE PROBLEM (0:45 – 1:45 | 60 seconds)**
> **[Show a student facing a choice between a mission screen, a book, and a video.]**
> *"Space education often misses the middle. At one extreme, cartoons remove the engineering and consequences. At the other, technical material can overwhelm a beginner. And a single format—read this, then take a quiz—doesn’t work for every learner. Some students understand by playing; others need to hear, see, read, or write. Most importantly, students need room to fail, understand why, and try again. Real mission teams do exactly that: they use evidence, adjust a plan, and learn from the outcome. We asked: what if the mission became the classroom, with more than one way into every idea?"*

---

### **BLOCK 3: WHAT & THE WORKING DEMO (1:45 – 2:45 | 60 seconds)**
> **[Live demo: open a campaign mission, show its briefing, fly a short attempt, then show the debrief.]**
> *"Our Python and Pygame flight game runs at 60 frames per second and draws on a catalog of 76 historical NASA missions. Students take on six kinds of challenges: launch, docking, landing, rover, telescope, and deep space. Each mission begins with a briefing: the vehicle, objective, and conditions for success. During flight, students manage choices like thrust, trajectory, and fuel. The debrief shows the result and helps them try again with a better plan. Our React and TypeScript companion extends the challenge into resource management: students plan power, water, oxygen, and food across four survival missions."*

---

### **BLOCK 4: SO WHAT NEXT? IMPACT & OUR ASK (2:45 – 4:00 | 75 seconds)**
> **[Show the notebook, comic, audio, and video learning options; finish on the working app and team.]**
> *"The learning continues after flight. Students can explore a comic, listen to an audio lesson, watch a short video, or write in a handwritten-style mission notebook. These are not separate products; they are different ways into the same idea and back to the mission. Our scenarios connect to NASA sources, including the Baseline Values and Assumptions Document for life support, LRO’s LOLA elevation data, the KRUSTY Kilopower tests, and CRaTER radiation measurements. Today, the campaign, briefings, debriefs, resource management, four survival missions, and learning formats are ready to explore. Next, we want to pilot with teachers, improve classroom and multilingual access, and make mission feedback even clearer. We need [teachers to pilot it / access to more NASA mission archives / partners for learning content]. The next astronaut may first learn to make a mission decision here. We are Team Mysterio. Jr_AstroCamp: Fly. Learn. Explore. Thank you."*

## 📑 7-Slide Deck Blueprint (NASA Accepted Alternative)
If submitting the official 7-slide deck alternative instead of Video 3:
1. **Slide 1: Title & Challenge**: Jr_AstroCamp: Beyond Earth | Challenge: Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer | Team Mysterio.
