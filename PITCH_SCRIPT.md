# Jr_AstroCamp — Video 2 Script (Browser Version)

**Team Mysterio | NASA International Space Apps Challenge 2026 | Local Judging: 240 Seconds of Glory (under 4:00)**

This script covers the browser app only. It follows the 45/60/60/75 structure: WHO, WHY, WHAT, SO WHAT NEXT.

## Block 1: WHO (0:00–0:45)

**On camera:** the team, then the Jr_AstroCamp title.

“Hello everyone, welcome to the NASA International Space Apps Challenge 2026!”

“Becoming an astronaut used to mean waiting decades for a seat on a real mission. What if you could fly one today? And what if, when you crash, you could simply try again?”

“We are Team Mysterio, and this is Jr_AstroCamp: an ed-tech platform built around a game, where students don’t just read about real NASA missions. They fly them, and then learn about them in the way that works best for them.”

“We’re six people, including an AI automation engineer, a data scientist, an ML engineer, a full-stack developer, a researcher, and a UI/UX designer. Different skills, one shared memory: looking up at the night sky and wondering, what it would take to get up there.”

## Block 2: WHY (0:45–1:45)

**Show:** a student choosing between a mission screen, a book, and a video.

“Space education often misses the middle. At one extreme, cartoons remove the engineering and the consequences. At the other, technical material can overwhelm a beginner. And a single format, read this and take a quiz, doesn't work for every learner. Some students understand by playing, others need to hear, see, read, or write. Most importantly, students need room to fail, understand why, and try again. Real mission teams do exactly that: they use evidence, adjust a plan, and learn from the outcome. So we asked: what if the mission became the classroom, with more than one way into every idea?”

## Block 3: WHAT, the working demo (1:45–2:45)

**Demo:** open the campaign map and a mission briefing, make one decision, show the debrief. Then one survival mission (Moon or Mars), click Start Learning and show its five options (video, audio, comic, handwritten notes, gameplay), the NASA Live Observatory, image search, and the Moonquake Map.

“Jr_AstroCamp is a mission game and learning platform, built with React and TypeScript, on 76 historical NASA missions. Students take on launch, docking, landing, rover, telescope, and deep-space challenges, with a briefing before each mission and a debrief after. Four survival missions based on NASA’s historical data, two on the Moon and two on Mars, have students manage oxygen, water, food, and power. When a student clicks Start Learning, five options appear: a short video, an audio lesson, a comic that tells the real story, handwritten-style notes, and gameplay. The NASA Live Observatory shows today’s Astronomy Picture of the Day and NeoWs asteroids, and image search uses NASA’s Image and Video Library. The Moonquake Map plots Apollo seismic events from Nakamura, 1979, with an illustrative waveform, not recorded telemetry.”

## Block 4: SO WHAT NEXT (2:45–4:00)

**Show:** the roadmap while talking about next steps. Put the named NASA sources on screen while speaking. Finish on the working app and the team.

“The science behind it comes from NASA sources. NASA's BVAD report informs the simplified oxygen, water, and food decisions in the habitat. We also cite LOLA, LCROSS, DONKI, CRaTER, REMS and MEDA, MOLA, and KRUSTY as references for lunar terrain and ice, solar storms and radiation, Mars conditions, and power. These are simplified scenario references, not raw live feeds. Our CapCom helper uses local response rules and authored science notes, and it does not send student questions to an external AI service. Next, we want to pilot with teachers, improve classroom access, and make mission feedback clearer. We're asking teachers to try the build with a class and share feedback. The next astronaut may first learn to make a mission decision here. We are Team Mysterio. Jr_AstroCamp: Fly. Learn. Explore. Thank you.”

## Notes before you record

- Only say what you show. Make sure each thing in Block 3 appears in the demo in that order: mission game, survival missions, the five Start Learning options, Live Observatory, image search, Moonquake Map.
