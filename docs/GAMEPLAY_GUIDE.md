## 1. Quick Start Overview

Jr_AstroCamp places you in the flight boots of an Astronaut Candidate (ASCAN) training at Houston Mission Control before deploying to manage humanity's first permanent lunar or Martian outpost.

### Core Loop:
1. **Train** in the **Astronaut Academy** (8 interactive ASCAN lessons with quizzes and interactive engineering widgets).
2. **Select Expedition Destination**: Moon (Shackleton Polar Base) or Mars (Jezero Crater Outpost).
3. **Allocate Outpost Cargo Budget**: Spend up to 45,000 kg (Moon) or 60,000 kg (Mars) across 10 critical systems.
4. **Command Daily Shifts (Sol 1–30)**: Balance power, water, oxygen, food, radiation shielding, and science collection.
5. **Respond to Consequential Mission Events**: Weather alerts, system faults, and unexpected discoveries with branching consequences.
6. **Drive the Surface Rover EVA**: Traverse lunar craters, avoid boulders, navigate by radar, and drill volatile water-ice cores.
7. **Complete Mission & Review Debrief**: Analyze the Explainable AI Mission Autopsy, crew safety, and NASA engineering recommendations.

## 2. Phase 1: Astronaut Academy (Training)

Before launching, cadets are encouraged to complete the 8-stage ASCAN curriculum in the Academy:
- **Module 1: Airlock & Depressurization Safety** — Delta-P atmospheric pressure differentials, pre-breathing 100% O2 to avoid decompression sickness (the bends).
- **Module 2: ECLSS & Sabatier Reactions** — CO2 + 4H2 -> CH4 + 2H2O stoichiometry; closing the oxygen and water loops.
- **Module 3: Power: Photovoltaics vs Fission** — Solar array sun-tracking angles vs 24/7 continuous Kilopower nuclear generation during the 354-hour lunar night.
- **Module 4: Potable Water Recycling** — Urine Processor Assembly (UPA) vacuum distillation and multi-barrier thermoelectric membrane filtration.
- **Module 5: Hydroponic Biomass & Nutrition** — Microgreen salad pods vs closed-loop biomes; light spectra (450nm blue / 660nm red LED) and transpiration recovery.
- **Module 6: Space Radiation & Regolith Shielding** — Galactic Cosmic Rays (GCR), Solar Particle Events (SPE), ALARA protocols, and 1.5m sintered lunar regolith storm vaults.
- **Module 7: Rover Traction & Terramechanics** — Lunar regolith shear strength, wheel slip ratio, obstacle avoidance, and radar waypoint tracking.
- **Module 8: Cryogenic Core Sample Collection** — Raman spectrometer analysis, volatile ice identification in permanently shadowed craters (<40 K).

*Cadets earn +50 to +100 XP per lesson and unlock the Mission Commander Gold Badge upon completing all 8 modules.*

## 3. Phase 2: Outpost Construction & Cargo Budget

You are given a strict launch mass budget on the heavy-lift booster:
- **Moon Budget:** `45,000 kg`
- **Mars Budget:** `60,000 kg`

You must select exactly one module for each of the 10 subsystems:
1. **Habitat Core:** Inflatable TransHab (8,000 kg) vs Heavy Regolith Dome (14,000 kg).
2. **Power Generation:** Foldable PV Array (4,000 kg) vs Polar Vertical Solar Towers (7,000 kg).
3. **Energy Storage:** Li-Ion Battery Bank (5,000 kg) vs Kilopower Fission Reactor (8,500 kg).
4. **Life Support (ECLSS):** Standard Physical-Chemical (4,500 kg) vs Closed-Loop Sabatier (8,000 kg).
5. **Water Recycling:** Vacuum Distillation (3,500 kg) vs Thermoelectric Membrane (6,500 kg).
6. **Greenhouse:** Compact Salad Pod (4,000 kg) vs Full Crop Biome (7,500 kg).
7. **Radiation Shielding:** Polyethylene Liners (3,000 kg) vs Sintered Regolith Vault (6,000 kg).
8. **Communications:** High-Gain S-Band Dish (2,500 kg) vs Deep Space Optical Laser DSOC (4,500 kg).
9. **Maintenance:** Manual Tool Locker (2,000 kg) vs Automated 3D Metal Fabricator (4,500 kg).
10. **Surface Rover:** Unpressurized LTV (3,000 kg) vs Pressurized Science Rover (6,500 kg).

**The Golden Trade-off:**
Selecting heavy, high-tech systems (e.g. Kilopower Reactor + Heavy Dome + Sabatier + Full Biome) will exceed your 45,000 kg rocket capacity, locking the launch button. You must make pragmatic engineering trade-offs!

## 4. Phase 3: Mission Control Simulation

Once landed on Sol 1, you manage the base shift by shift through Sol 30:

### Vital Life Support Indicators:
- **Power (0–100%):** Dips during night hours and high-load shifts. If battery reaches 0%, thermal heaters freeze, triggering mission failure.
- **Water (0–100%):** Consumed daily by the crew. High-efficiency recycling recovers up to 98% of graywater.
- **Oxygen (0–100%):** Consumed at 0.84 kg/crew-day. Electrolysis and Sabatier units maintain breathable partial pressure (21 kPa O2).
- **Food (Sols of Rations):** Greenhouses yield fresh biomass to replenish emergency ration stores.
- **Radiation (Cumulative mSv):** Must be kept below 100 mSv. Sintered regolith shielding slows daily accumulation.
- **Science (Points):** Goal: Reach 300+ research points through shifts, events, and rover excursions.
- **Crew Health (0–100%):** Reflects nutrition, environmental comfort, radiation exposure, and fatigue.

### Operational Shift Directives:
- **Standard Operations:** Balanced resource consumption and steady science yield (+2 science).
- **Power Conservation:** Reduces base electrical draw by 40%, dimming non-essential lighting and pausing scientific computers.
- **Science Intensive:** Ramps up laboratory spectrometers (+10 science) at the cost of +10 kW electrical load.
- **ECLSS Maintenance:** Technicians overhaul water filters and O2 scrubbers, improving recycling efficiency.
- **Greenhouse Surge:** Diverts +8 kW of supplemental LED lighting to accelerate plant growth cycles.
- **Deploy Rover EVA:** Launches an expedition into nearby craters to collect volatile ice samples.

## 5. Phase 4: Rover Exploration Minigame

Click **ROVER EVA** in the top navigation bar or trigger a surface exploration shift to launch the top-down rover mission:
- **Desktop Controls:**
  - `W` or `Up Arrow`: Drive forward (thrusters/electric motor)
  - `S` or `Down Arrow`: Reverse / brake
  - `A` or `Left Arrow`: Steer left
  - `D` or `Right Arrow`: Steer right
  - `Space`: Activate volatile core sample drill (when positioned over ice hotspot)
- **Touch / On-Screen Controls:**
  - Dedicated on-screen D-Pad buttons for tablets and mobile devices.
- **Navigation:**
  - Follow the green radar compass indicator to locate glowing volatile ice hotspots.
  - Avoid dark crater hazards and high-relief boulders that slow the rover down and deplete battery reserves.
  - Drill ice cores to earn +25 to +50 Science Research Points and return safely to the outpost airlock!

## 6. Phase 5: Mission Autopsy & Scoring

When you reach Sol 30 or experience a catastrophic system failure:
- **Outcomes:**
  - `VICTORY`: All crew members survived Sol 30 with >300 Science Points collected.
  - `PARTIAL_SUCCESS`: Crew survived Sol 30, but resource reserves ran dangerously low or science targets were unmet.
  - `CRITICAL_FAILURE`: Hypoxia (O2 exhausted), Thermal Freeze (Battery 0%), or Acute Radiation (>100 mSv).
- **Explainable AI Root-Cause Tree:**
  - Traces the exact chain reaction from early decisions to final outcomes (e.g., *Neglecting water recycling on Sol 8 forced battery power diversion on Sol 16, resulting in greenhouse heating failure on Sol 22*).
- **NASA Engineering Recommendations:** Actionable tips for your next mission attempt.
